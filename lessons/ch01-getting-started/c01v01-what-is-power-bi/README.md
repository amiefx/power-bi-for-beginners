# Lesson 1.1 — What is Power BI?

▶️ **Video:** <video link>

Contoso has about 224,000 sales in many files. Sara, the Sales Manager, asks: "How can I see what's happening?" In this lesson you learn what business intelligence is, what Power BI does, and what's inside a report.

## What you'll learn
- **Data:** facts and numbers, for example every sale
- **Business intelligence (BI):** using data to understand a business and make better decisions
- **Power BI:** a Microsoft tool that turns data into reports
- **Visual:** one chart, table or number on a report page
- **Report:** one or more pages of visuals that answer questions about data
- The map of this course: load → clean → connect → calculate → build visuals → share

## Files
This lesson has no clicks in Power BI, so there is no start or end file. To get ready for the next lessons, follow the setup in the [main README](../../../README.md).

## The lesson in short
1. Contoso, a shop that sells electronics and more, has data on sales, products, customers and stores. You are the new analyst on Sara's team.
2. Lines of data alone don't answer Sara's questions ("What sells best? Where are sales falling?"). Business intelligence uses data to answer them.
3. Power BI turns data into reports. You build them in a free app on your computer, share them on a website, and people can view them in a phone app (lesson 1.2 shows each part).
4. A report page has visuals: a big number with all sales, a chart by category and a chart by country. Together they answer Sara's question in a few seconds: almost $219 million of sales, and Computers sold the most.

⚠️ **Common mistake:** jumping straight to the charts. A big part of the work comes before them: loading, cleaning, connecting and calculating.

## Your turn
Pick a shop you know. What data does it collect? And what's a question a report could answer for the shop's manager?

<details>
<summary>Example answer</summary>

There is no single right answer. Here is an example. A phone shop collects data on every sale (the day, the product, the price and how many), on its products (name, brand, price) and on its customers. A question a report could answer for the shop's manager: "Which product sold best this month?" A chart by product shows it. Another one: "How much did we sell this month?" One big number shows it.

Check your own answer: is your data facts and numbers the shop collects? And does your question help the manager make a better decision? Then it's business intelligence.
</details>
